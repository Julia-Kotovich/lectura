# Human Evaluation — Wave 1 vs Wave 2

Comparison of two rounds of human evaluation for the MCSSE-SE-01 lecture decks.

## Short summary

The overall average remained almost unchanged between the two rounds: **2.78/5** in Wave 1 and **2.80/5** in Wave 2. Wave 2 shows a small improvement in **Completeness**, **Clarity**, and **Ready-to-teach**, while **Correctness** became slightly lower. Usability judgments also remained similar: no lecture received a majority **Yes** in either round, and most decks were still judged as requiring substantial editing.

## Main takeaways

- The **overall average** is effectively stable across the two waves.
- Wave 2 is slightly better in **Clarity** and **Ready-to-teach**, which suggests some improvement in presentation and teachability.
- The strongest criterion in both waves is still **Correctness**, although it is lower in Wave 2.
- In both waves, **no lecture** reached majority **Yes** for usability.
- Wave 2 shows small usability gains for **Lectures 1 and 7**, while **Lecture 9** moved in the opposite direction.

## One-paragraph version for the paper

Comparing the two rounds of human evaluation, the overall mean score remained nearly unchanged, rising only from **2.78/5** in Wave 1 to **2.80/5** in Wave 2. At the criterion level, Wave 2 showed modest gains in **Completeness** (+0.03), **Clarity** (+0.16), and **Ready-to-teach** (+0.14), while **Correctness** decreased slightly (-0.26). Usability judgments remained broadly similar: **No** accounted for 18 votes in Wave 1 and 17 in Wave 2, **With edits** increased from 12 to 13, and **Yes** remained at 0 in both rounds. Overall, the second wave suggests limited improvement in teachability and readability, but not a qualitative shift toward classroom-ready lecture decks.
